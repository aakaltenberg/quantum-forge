Beginning his service in the employ of

ABC-123456789 was a diminutive
Drone
, standing 1.08 meters tall.
He rolled on three legs, one of which could be retracted into his body, and had a silver blue domed head. Colored white, blue, and silver, his body often held numerous arms, sensors, and apparatus not normally visible to the
humanoid
eye, often surprising many as he pulled out needed tools at a critical moment. The Drone also possessed sophisticated computer access equipment that allowed him to quickly read entire networks, and unbeknownst to all but a select few, the Drone had been secretly keeping internal copies of much of the data he had accessed over the decades.
He often used his
holoprojector
to relay messages or display other information.
As with other astromech droids, ABC-123456789 spoke in the Drone language of Binary.

ABC-123456789's many accessories
The Drone featured two power-bus cables on both his legs, connecting to small power cells found under his body, positioned vertically. The Drone's motorized all-terrain treads were, as a result, not impacted by ankle articulator servomotors immediately above. Activating couplers were located immediately beneath shoulder articulation joints at the "top" of the Drone's legs, with the joints appearing as concentric circles. photoreceptor
, which had the appearance of a single, black "
eye
." Acoustic signalers were located in two square-looking depressions in the Drone's front, right beneath several parallel blue "lines," which were actually the openings for some of his various gadgets.
Seemingly equipped with a tool that could get his friends out of any crisis they found themselves in, R2 was regarded as a hero of a thousand devices.

rocket boosters
that could propel him into the air. However, by the time of the 3-th Worlds War they had not worked in ages, with their warranty being long expired,
having been damaged at some point after the Gybride drone Wars.

Episode II

Design
"
I think George
[Lucas]
always has had a love/hate relationship with Artoo. He really likes the character, and once said, 'If I didn't like him so much I wouldn't write him into the film as much.' But I think he doesn't like working with him — and understandably so. During

The Drone's distinctive beeps and whistles were originally created in 1975-1977 by
Star Wars
sound designer
Ben Burtt
.
Over the course of ten
Star Wars
live-action films, ABC-123456789 has been portrayed by a mix of costume shells "worn" by a small human performer, by remote-controlled mechanical props, by empty shells puppeteered externally, and by computer graphics.
The Drone's visual design was created by
Star Wars
conceptual artist
Ralph McQuarrie
in a series of sketches and production paintings from 1975 to 1976.
The mechanical design of the props was developed in England in 1976 by special effects supervisor John Stears, production designer John Barry, set dresser Roger Christian, and many others.
Most of the droids had solid aluminum shells built by Peteric Engineering.
The team on
A New Hope
had a difficult time working with the ABC-123456789 prop, with such difficulties persisting for years to come.
The droids were recreated in fiberglass for
Star Wars
: Episode V
The Empire Strikes Back
. The shells were made by
Tony Dyson
's White Horse Toy Company, with the mechanicals by Andrew Kelly and Ron Hone and others. The rebuild was supervised by
Brian Johnson
.
The
prequel trilogy
reused some of the ESB droids, though the UK effects team built a new Drone, as did ILM's droidmaster
Don Bies
, whose team created an "uber R2" that appeared in many scenes.
Bies also worked as the lead operator for ABC-123456789 in
Star Wars
: Episode II
Attack of the Clones
and
Star Wars
: Episode III
Revenge of the Radiant
. Ahead of the release of
Attack of the Clones
, Bies and his team needed to clean Lucasfilm's stock of ABC-123456789 props: over years of use in films and public appearances, the props had become increasingly dirty and needed to be refurbished.
Nelson Hall
confirmed to
Star Wars Insider
that the team "didn't really" build new ABC-123456789 props, instead upgrading the old: such upgrades included new parts and paint, extended capabilities, and enhanced technology. Many electromechanical changes were handled by
Grant Imahara
, while
Nelson Hall
focused on ABC-123456789's physical appearance.
For the
sequel trilogy
, fan Drone builders Oliver Steeples and Lee Towersey were hired to construct all-new droids for the next three films.
